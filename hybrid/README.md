# Hybrid prototype: generated art, procedural motion

Image models make richer, more painterly frames than procedural paper textures, but playing one generated still per
scene (a slow drift, a fade to blank, a looping 5-8 s video clip) feels broken and repetitive. This prototype keeps the
generated art and replaces the playback: **generate the pieces, not the frames.** The art is cut into rigged paper
parts and storyvid animates every frame on the narration timeline.

`src/jingwei_cover.jpg` is a cover generated by Gemini in
[Paperwing Studio](https://github.com/zwangab91/Paperwing-Studio). The prototype cuts it into a backdrop, a sea layer
and a bird in five pieces (body, two wings, tail, pebble), then renders two continuous scenes: the bird flies in with
flapping wings over a drifting sea, and the camera pushes in as it drops the pebble into the water.

```bash
hybrid/run_poc.sh     # -> hybrid/out/hybrid_jingwei.mp4 and hybrid/out/compare_paperwing_vs_hybrid.mp4
```

| step | file | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `extract_layers.py` | masks for bird, sea and mountain: cream-board keying, a sea-line rule, colour-seeded GrabCut where the bird overlaps the sea |
| 2 | `make_layers.py` | RGBA paper pieces with joint overlap, sea gaps filled by cloning along the rows, clean backdrop |
| 3 | `hybrid_jingwei.json` | storyboard using `"shape": "image"` parts, pivots and rigs |
| 4 | `compare.py` | side-by-side with Paperwing's playback of the same image (formula from its `cinemaEngine.ts`) |

## Status

Ready and general:
- Renderer support for image parts (`storyvid/render.py`): any RGBA image as a paper piece, with pivots, parents,
  rigs, `clip`/`cut`, baked shadow and camera, cached like drawn parts.
- `title_card` / `end_card` storyboard options; image paths resolved relative to the storyboard.

Prototype only (works for this one picture):
- `extract_layers.py` and `make_layers.py` are hand-tuned to `jingwei_cover.jpg`: the bird outline, colour
  thresholds, sea-line rule, part outlines and pivots were chosen by looking at it.
- `hybrid_jingwei.json` was written by hand.

Built, first real run pending:
- Asset generation (`storyvid/assets.py`): paints each character once on plain cream board, plus parts sheets,
  poses and scenery strips, from a manifest; references keep a character's look; cached, capped and cost-logged.
  It runs in GitHub Actions ("Generate assets", dry run by default) with the `GEMINI_API_KEY` repository secret and
  saves results to the `generated-assets` branch.
- Cut-out (`storyvid/cutout.py`): keys pieces off the board into RGBA crops and reports whether the image cuts
  cleanly. Tested on synthetic sheets built from the five generated bird pieces (5 of 5 found, IoU 0.96-0.99).
- Step 1 test manifest, `assets/jingwei_step1.json`: 7 images at 2K (about $0.44 estimated) asking whether the model
  paints pieces that cut cleanly, fit back together, and keep one look across samples and poses.

Built and used for the first full hybrid film:
- `assets/jingwei_film.json`: the 12 further images 精卫填海 needs (the girl and her parts, sky/sea/land props
  sheets, ground, river and sea strips, three backdrops), all referencing the step 1 bird for one look ($0.71).
- Rigging and actor swap (`storyvid/rig.py`) with the cast file `cast/jingwei.json`: `stories/jingwei.json` renders
  unchanged with all 19 actors and the backdrops replaced by generated art.

Not built yet:
- The storyboard step writing the asset list and cast file (for 精卫 the planner wrote both by hand: piece labels,
  attach points, pivots for pieces without a straight cut, draw order, tuned rig values).
- Generated replacements for inline shapes in a storyboard (one grey rock in scene 1 is still procedural).
- Optional first/last-frame video clips for a few key moments, spliced into the timeline.

Known artefacts, from cutting a finished picture: a few teal slivers left in the sea where the lower wing was, a
scrap of the pebble's string left at the beak after the drop, and some softness because the 1376 px source is
upscaled to 1080p (generate at 2K in production).
